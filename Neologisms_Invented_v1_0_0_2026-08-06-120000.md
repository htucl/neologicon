## Physical Sensations & Tactile

**flatch** - a flat surface that should have texture but doesn't, creating an unsatisfying feeling.
*Example: "This supposedly ergonomic mouse is just flatch plastic."*

**grindle** - hair wrapped tightly around a finger or object, cutting off circulation.
*Example: "I've got a grindle—can you help me unwrap this?"*

**fratch** - static cling between two pieces of fabric that resists separation.
*Example: "These sheets have fratch—they won't separate."*

**flibber** - the moment your legs give out slightly when standing still for too long, a brief loss of support sensation.
*Example: "My knees started to flibber during the ceremony."*

**grawp** - the sensation of fabric bunching uncomfortably in the exact spot you can't reach to adjust.
*Example: "I have a grawp in my shirt that I can't fix from the back."*

**splurch** - the unsatisfying feeling of stepping in a puddle while wearing socks.
*Example: "I felt a splurch and immediately regretted wearing sandals with no shoes."*

**knarch** - the specific frustration of something being just barely out of reach.
*Example: "The remote is in knarch distance—so close but useless."*

**vrintz** - pins-and-needles sensation when your foot or limb falls asleep.
*Example: "My leg is covered in vrintz after sitting wrong for an hour."*

**flurm** - the warmth and slight heaviness of a cat sitting on your lap.
*Example: "There's nothing better than the flurm of a contented cat."*

## Sounds & Onomatopoeia

**splunge** - the soft, slightly disgusting sound of stepping in something wet and unknown.
*Example: "I heard a splunge and immediately regretted wearing sandals."*

**tronk** - the hollow metallic sound something makes when it's broken but you're not sure how.
*Example: "The engine made a tronk and the car died."*

**thwink** - the exact sound of a rubber band snapping back into place.
*Example: "The thwink of the slingshot echoed across the yard."*

**schnarb** - to eat messily and unapologetically, making audible sounds.
*Example: "He schnarbed his way through the popcorn."*

**thrump** - the dull, heavy sound of something soft landing on something hard.
*Example: "The pillow made a soft thrump when it hit the floor."*

**mrawl** - the sound a cat makes when it wants something specific and won't stop.
*Example: "The cat's mrawl means it's dinner time."*

**snorble** - to breathe heavily through your nose while trying to stay quiet, often suppressed laughter.
*Example: "I snorbled so hard during the meeting I had to fake a cough."*

**glorf** - a laugh-snort hybrid where you partially succeed in holding it in.
*Example: "I glorffed so hard during the meeting everyone turned to look."*

**grawnt** - the low, guttural sound of effort when lifting something heavy.
*Example: "He let out a grawnt as he heaved the box onto the shelf."*

**vrawl** - to speak while your mouth is too full to articulate properly.
*Example: "He vrawled something incomprehensible through his sandwich."*

## Emotional & Social Moments

**vrinch** - a full-body shudder triggered by something deeply unpleasant (nails on chalkboard, certain voices).
*Example: "His nasally laugh gave me an immediate vrinch."*

**plinch** - the specific guilt of laughing at something you're not supposed to find funny.
*Example: "I felt a plinch of guilt for plinching at his misfortune."*

**klench** - the jaw-clench moment when you remember something embarrassing you did years ago.
*Example: "I had a sudden klench remembering that thing I said in third grade."*

**klinch** - the moment you lock eyes with a stranger and both realize you've made contact; mutual recognition of the awkwardness.
*Example: "We had a klinch in the elevator and both looked away immediately."*

**grintz** - the uncomfortable silence after someone tells a joke that doesn't land.
*Example: "His joke fell flat and we all sat in grintz."*

**brinch** - that sinking realization mid-sentence that you're about to say something you shouldn't.
*Example: "I had a brinch right before I almost insulted her outfit."*

**splint** - the specific loneliness of eating alone in public.
*Example: "I felt a splint sitting at the restaurant table by myself."*

**gnarl** - to complain while not expecting anyone to actually fix it; venting without hope.
*Example: "He's been gnarling about the weather all week."*

**snirk** - to smile while simultaneously being skeptical about what you're hearing.
*Example: "She snirked at his excuse, unconvinced."*

**throld** - the threshold of annoyance where you stop being patient and start being visibly irritated.
*Example: "He crossed my throld when he interrupted for the third time."*

## Bodily States & Sensations

**bretch** - that moment when you almost sneeze but it doesn't complete, leaving you suspended in anticipation.
*Example: "I had a bretch and then nothing—now I'm uncomfortable."*

**frowth** - that small, involuntary groan when you realize you have to get up.
*Example: "I let out a frowth when the alarm went off."*

**brawth** - a deep, satisfied sigh after finishing something difficult.
*Example: "I brawthd as I submitted the final draft."*

**flunch** - that particular exhaustion after a large meal.
*Example: "I'm experiencing flunch and need to lie down."*

**whimpel** - when a dog or person makes a frustrated noise in the throat without fully committing to a whine.
*Example: "The toddler whimpeled when she couldn't reach the toy."*

**shrinch** - to physically recoil from something disgusting, distinct from flinching.
*Example: "I shrinched when I saw the wound."*

**natch** - the small, satisfied sound you make when you finally crack your neck or knuckles (distinct from the crack itself).
*Example: "He let out a natch of satisfaction."*

## Visual & Perceptual

**mirl** - to stare at something without actually seeing it; mental absence while looking directly at something.
*Example: "I mirl at my phone screen for ten minutes without reading anything."*

**vetch** - the feeling of déjà vu but backwards—being somewhere familiar and it feels completely foreign.
*Example: "I had a vetch moment in my childhood home; nothing felt right."*

**scrawm** - to search frantically through a pile for one specific item.
*Example: "I've been scrawming through my files for the contract all morning."*

**trawl** - the exhausting mental effort of remembering something on the tip of your tongue.
*Example: "I'm trawling for the name of that actor."*

**tronch** - the moment a light bulb flickers and you hold your breath waiting to see if it dies.
*Example: "The bulb had a tronch and I wasn't sure if it would survive."*

**spronk** - the moment an object tips just far enough to be genuinely unstable but hasn't fallen yet.
*Example: "The glass was in spronk; one more vibration and it would have crashed."*

## Environmental & Sensory Phenomena

**snirp** - water or liquid flowing upward, or more generally, anything moving counter to expected gravity or flow.
*Example: "The fountain snirps gracefully into the basin."*

**brindle** - the precise moment when hot tea becomes drinkable temperature, cooling from steaming to warm.
*Example: "Wait for the tea to reach brindle before drinking it."*

**swail** - the sag of expensive furniture that's still under warranty, premature settling.
*Example: "Even the luxury sofa started to swail after six months."*

**shrent** - the particular sadness of a plant dying on your windowsill despite your efforts.
*Example: "I felt a wave of shrent watching my orchid wilt."*

**sprent** - the smell of rain on hot pavement; that distinctive petrichor scent.
*Example: "The sprent after the thunderstorm was incredible."*

**brintz** - the metallic taste in your mouth after eating something acidic (citrus, vinegar).
*Example: "I have brintz on my tongue from the lemon water."*

**glunk** - the specific sluggishness of something that almost works but not quite; lagging performance.
*Example: "My computer's been glunk all morning."*

**thrip** - a tiny, insignificant problem that accumulates into something annoying (paper cuts, lost socks, dead pixels).
*Example: "It's just thrips—nothing catastrophic but everything adds up."*

**snapniff** - the involuntary inhalation when you catch an unexpectedly good smell.
*Example: "She caught a snapniff of fresh bread from the bakery."*

**flich** - the tiny, sharp pain of a paper cut.
*Example: "A flich from the envelope made me wince."*

**knurl** - the instinctive wince you make hearing someone's nails on a surface.
*Example: "I knurled at the sound of nails on the chalkboard."*

**knarl** - a knot in wood that sticks out just enough to catch your sleeve or skin.
*Example: "That knarl on the door frame always snags my shirt."*

**vrill** - that electric feeling of anticipation right before something exciting happens.
*Example: "I felt a vrill as the concert was about to start."*

## Notes

These 59 neologisms were invented during a single conversation exploring the question: *What simple words should have been invented a long time ago?* The criterion for inclusion was that each word should feel obvious in retrospect—as though it fills a gap in English vocabulary that genuinely existed.

The strongest candidates are those that describe specific, un-named experiences: **vrinch** (full-body shudder), **splunge** (wet step sound), **tronk** (broken metallic sound), **mirl** (mindless staring), and **sprent** (petrichor smell) exemplify the ideal. Weaker entries may be too close to obvious portmanteaus or existing words.

All definitions were generated during the conversation and should be considered provisional. Actual utility and adoption by speakers would validate these as genuine neologisms.
